    About

    The continents move by the PALEOMAP plate model, and the land and sea floor come from its paleo-elevation models (Scotese and Wright 2018), one every 5 million years, blended between steps. A present-day place rides the plate that holds it; recon.js reproduces pygplates to within a metre.

    Today's Earth is NASA's Blue Marble with the Black Marble city lights. For past ages, the colours of land, the ice and the clouds are an estimate made for this page: a global temperature curve, latitude, height and distance from the sea give each place a yearly temperature and wetness, and it takes the mean Blue Marble colour of present-day land with the same climate. They show the kind of world, not a map of real forests. The clouds drift with the prevailing winds, and while time plays they churn faster, so a million years of weather pass in a blink; their pattern is illustrative. Land plants spread from about 470 Ma, forests from about 385 Ma, grasslands in the last 25 million years.

    Longitude is not fixed by paleomagnetism, so the east–west position of old continents is a model choice.

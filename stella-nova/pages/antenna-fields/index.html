The current on the wire

A centre-fed wire carries a standing wave of current. It is zero at the open ends. The gold curve beside each wire is the current now; the faint band is its envelope. Each short piece of current radiates, and the field on screen is the sum of all of them.

For the Yagi-Uda only the middle wire is fed. The currents on the other wires come from the moment method: the field of all the currents together must be zero along every wire.

Near field and far field

The field of one short piece of current has three terms. Close to the wire the 1/r³ and 1/r² terms win: the field stores energy and gives it back each cycle, and E is 90° behind B. Far away only the 1/r term is left: E and B are across the radius, in phase, and E = cB. That part carries energy away. The change happens near kr = 1, one sixth of a wavelength out. Drag the marker outward and read the phase in its label.

Energy flow

The Poynting vector S is the flow of energy: power per unit area, along E × B. With Energy flow on, particles move with the energy velocity S/u. Far away they move out at the speed of light with the wavefronts. Near the wire they move out and back: that is the stored energy. The speed can never pass c.

Flux through a sphere

The dashed circle is a sphere around the antenna, through the marker, cut by the plane. Its arrows show S · n now: out, or back in. Add the flux over the whole sphere and you get the power that leaves. In empty space the time-average flow has no divergence, so the divergence theorem gives the same flux through every sphere: drag the marker and the average stays the same. The flux now swings around that average, and near the antenna it goes below zero.

The radiation pattern

Far away, the power per solid angle U depends only on the direction. In the 3D view its radius in each direction is the field strength there. The same function on a sphere is the flux density: the 3D option changes one shape into the other. Directivity compares the strongest direction with the average.

A half-wave dipole has D = 1.64 (2.15 dBi): a ring of power around the wire and none along it.

Arrays and the phasor sum

The far field toward one direction is a sum of arrows, one per element: the length is the current, the angle is the phase of the current plus the extra path. The box at the lower right draws this sum head to tail toward the marker; the arrows turn together at the wave frequency. Where the arrows line up, the beam is; where they close into a polygon, there is a null. A phase step β from one element to the next turns the beam.

About

The field is exact for the current on each wire: a sinusoidal current between equally spaced nodes has a closed-form field, and the GPU adds it up for every pixel. The loop uses many short elements. The numbers come from the far-field sum and from the flux through a sphere; the two agree.

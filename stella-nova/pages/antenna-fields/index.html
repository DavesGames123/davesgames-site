About

A current that changes in time pushes on the field around it. Close to the wire the field stores energy and gives it back each cycle: the reactive near field, inside about λ/2π (or 0.62√(D³/λ) for a large antenna of size D). The cyan ring marks it. The orange ring marks the start of the far field, where the pattern no longer changes with distance: beyond 2D²/λ, 2D and λ. Farther out, closed loops of electric field break away from the wire and travel outward at the speed of light. That is radiation.

The page cuts the wire into short current elements and adds up the exact field of each one, with the 1/r, 1/r² and 1/r³ terms. The GPU does this sum for every pixel. The time animation is Re{E ejωt}.

The currents are not guessed. A dipole can use the textbook sinusoidal current, or a moment-method solution with piecewise-sinusoidal bases. A Yagi-Uda has one driven element. Its reflector and directors have no feed, and the moment method finds the currents that the near field induces in them. A loop is solved mode by mode in a Fourier series.

The field lines are exact where a flux function exists: for a dipole in the side view they are the contours of ρHφ. In the other views the page traces the field direction.

The idea

Most systems that let you walk through a scene from a few photos first build an explicit 3D model, a point cloud, a mesh, a radiance field or Gaussian splats, and then render it. AVM does not. It treats a scene as a stream of views: each view is a short video clip with a camera pose and a timestamp. Given some views as context and a camera path, it generates the next clip along the path, adds it to its history, and goes on.

Whether it reconstructs or invents is not a switch. Where the context shows the requested part of the scene, the output follows the context (reconstruction and interpolation). Where nothing in the context covers it, a learned video prior fills it in (extrapolation). The same interface handles single images, several posed images, a video, synchronised multi-camera video, and the model's own earlier output.

One model, N views in, one clip out

AVM fine-tunes a pretrained image-to-video diffusion transformer with 14 billion parameters. A spatiotemporal autoencoder turns video into latents, one latent per 8 × 8 pixel × 4 frame block. The transformer reads N context slots and one target slot as one sequence. Each slot gets its noisy latent, a reference latent and a mask: the context slots carry the clean latent with mask 1, the target slot carries zeros.

The camera enters in two places. Translation, in metric world units, goes in as rotary position embeddings on the attention queries and keys, so large coordinates stay in range. Rotation goes in as embeddings of the per-pixel camera rays, added to the patches. Timestamps get a sinusoidal embedding: constant for a static scene, rising for a moving one. Text prompts go through T5 and cross-attention.

Context images or clips of the same scene lose their timestamps and act like the examples in a language-model prompt: that is the "in-context" part. Synchronised streams from another camera keep the target's timestamps.

A bounded context

A long walk makes a long history, and attention over all of it would grow without end. AVM keeps the context for each target to a fixed size made of four parts, the parts of Figure 1:

Sink views are input views kept for the whole run, so the style and content of the input stay fixed. Retrieved views come from the pool of input and generated views by camera pose (or by timestamp for synced video). Motion views are the most recent frames, for smooth motion. The base model makes 19 latent frames per chunk after 2 frames of motion; the causal model makes 3 at a time after 15 and keeps the rest in a KV cache, which it can move to CPU memory and load back when a view is retrieved.

From slow and bidirectional to fast and causal

The base model sees its whole target clip at once and needs 50 Euler steps with classifier-free guidance:

For streaming, the authors turn it into a causal model with sparse attention. They start with a mix of teacher forcing (conditioning on clean earlier blocks) and diffusion forcing (on noisy ones), with error recycling that feeds the model its own stored mistakes. Then they tune it on its own rollouts with distribution-matching distillation and an adversarial loss, on rollouts that get longer over training, with the same prefill and history context as at inference. The result samples in 4 steps with the guidance folded in. The ablation shows why the last stage matters: the causal model before on-policy tuning reaches a revisit PSNR of only 10.12 dB.

Data

About 6 million labelled scenes with metric camera poses and captions (indoor and outdoor captures, posed web video, object videos, synthetic and game renderings, synchronised multi-camera video, cubemaps) and about 2 million unlabelled clips that keep the base model's motion and text following. Clips are five seconds long; resolution grows from 448 × 448 to 720 × 1280 during training. Image edits with FLUX teach the model to stay coherent when the context images disagree.

Limits

  • Text control is weak: 39.65 against 54.07 for the best world model, and a "garden party" prompt can still give an indoor room.
  • Motion is cautious: the lowest dynamic degree of the four models tested (24.88 against 35.55).
  • The authors say visual artifacts remain and expect quality to grow with model and data scale. Dense-capture prefill is an early result.
  • On 3D consistency for revisits, LingBot-World scores higher (57.00 against 53.13).
  • At the time of writing the project page links no code or weights.

The authors' next steps: stronger open-weight video priors, optional geometric proxies for finer control, and use inside agent workflows.

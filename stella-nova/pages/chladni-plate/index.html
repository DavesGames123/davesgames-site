How it works

A thin plate bends. Its free modes solve D∇⁴w = ρhω²w: a stiffness D against the mass ρh per area. Each mode has a shape φn and a frequency. The lines where φn = 0 are its nodal lines.

The page finds the lowest 24 modes of each outline with a finite-difference model on a grid of about 5000 points, in a background worker. Wood is stiffer along the grain than across it, so wood plates use the orthotropic form.

Driven at one point, the plate moves as a sum of all modes. Near a mode’s frequency that one mode wins, and the sand finds its nodal lines. Sand only jumps where the plate’s acceleration is more than g, so between resonances the pattern stays still.

The plates are flat and of one thickness. A real violin plate is arched and graduated, so “Arched” makes it stiffer across the grain as an estimate of the arch.

Why coral looks like DLA

Many corals feed on food that drifts in the water. Near the colony the water is slow, so the food reaches the coral mostly by diffusion, a random walk. A random walker that comes near a branched shape almost always touches a tip first: the gaps between branches are screened. So tips catch more food, grow faster, and stay ahead. That is diffusion-limited aggregation (DLA), and it is why a nutrient-limited coral grows branches.

A current changes the picture. The snow comes from one side, so the side that faces the current gets more and the colony grows upstream. Calm, food-rich water gives compact forms; thin food gives thin branches. Kaandorp and co-workers showed this with DLA in a flow (Kaandorp, Lowe, Frenkel and Sloot, Phys. Rev. Lett. 77, 2328, 1996), and grew whole colonies with an accretive model (Kaandorp and Kübler, The Algorithmic Beauty of Seaweeds, Sponges and Corals, 2001).

The real dlaf walk of one particle. Far from the cluster it jumps by its distance to the cluster, then it steps until it is near enough to join. It nearly always joins a tip.

Which dlaf hook makes which coral

StaghornshouldJoin refuses a join on a busy particle or from below, so tips grow up. Low AttractionDistance, high Stickiness.
Sea fanmotionVector and randomStartingPosition keep the walk in a vertical plane across the current.
TableshouldJoin lets a short stalk grow up, then only horizontal joins stick; placeParticle flattens the plate.
BrainHigh Stubbornness; placeParticle lays each polyp on a compact dome (accretive growth).
FingerLow Stickiness and a slow fall: walkers creep in, so the lobes get thick.
AllrandomStartingPosition puts the snow on a disc above the colony, upstream; motionVector adds the fall and the current; shouldReset starts again at the seabed or out of the reef box.

Colour by

Age is the order of joining, so the growing front is at the hot end of the gradient and the colours move while the reef grows. Depth counts the hops from the seed. Flow is the size of the subtree that a polyp feeds. Light is an estimate from the height and the number of close neighbours.

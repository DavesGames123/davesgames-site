Build the enzyme around the chemistry it has to do

An enzyme is mostly packaging. The chemistry happens at a handful of chemical groups held in one exact arrangement; the rest of the protein exists to hold them there. So you can turn enzyme making around: write down the arrangement first, then ask a model to grow a protein that holds it. AlphaProtein Novo does that. A diffusion model draws a backbone around the motif and the molecule it acts on, a second model may rewrite the sequence for that shape, AlphaFold 3 folds the sequence back, and a set of metrics asks one question: did the active site survive the round trip? The pipeline is built to generate far more designs than it keeps.

01

The problem: a few groups, held exactly

A reaction has a worst moment. Halfway through, bonds are half broken and charge sits where no stable molecule would put it. That state is the transition state, and it is rare, which is why uncatalysed reactions are slow. An enzyme works by making that moment cheaper: it surrounds the transition state with groups that fit it better than they fit the starting material.

What a catalytic motif is

our own drawing, not from the paper

The motif is the small set of atoms the reaction needs: here a base that takes a proton, a donor that steadies the negative charge that forms, and an anchor that holds the molecule still. What matters is not that the protein contains those groups but that it holds them at the right distances and angles from each other and from the substrate. Move one group by a fraction of a bond length and the catalysis goes. The rest of the chain, all of it, is scaffolding with one job: hold the motif.

Textbook relation, not a result of the paper: the speed-up is set by how much the enzyme lowers the barrier. The exponential is the whole difficulty. A small error in the arrangement costs a large factor in rate.

The old way needs a protein that already exists

background, our own words

Traditional enzyme engineering starts from a natural protein that already does something close to what you want, then improves it by rounds of mutation and selection. It works well, and it has one hard requirement: the starting protein. Someone has to find it, by searching sequence databases for a family that acts on a similar molecule by a similar mechanism.

For a reaction that nothing in nature performs, that search can come back empty. There is no family to mine and no scaffold to improve. The same wall appears when the substrate is man-made: a natural enzyme may use the right chemistry but have a pocket shaped for the wrong molecule, and widening a pocket without breaking the fold is its own hard problem.

De novo design removes the requirement. Instead of finding a protein and bending it toward the chemistry, you state the chemistry as a geometric arrangement and ask for a protein that holds it. Nothing in the method cares whether the reaction has a natural precedent. What the method needs instead is a way to know which of its many designs are worth making in the laboratory, because the arrangement being drawn correctly on a screen says nothing about whether real protein will hold it.

    03

    Scaffolding: a one-line language for the chain

    Before anything is generated, the designer writes down what the chain must contain and how much freedom it has. The repository does that with one string, motif_str. It lists segments in order: the motif residues, held at their input coordinates, separated by runs of residues the model is free to invent. A run written as a range is sampled once per design, so every design in a campaign gets a different layout.

    parsed by our own code in design.js

    Blocks on the bar are chain segments, left to right along the sequence. A held block is a motif residue kept at its input coordinates. A designed block is free chain, and a block written as a range is redrawn each time you change the seed: that is the only randomness in the layout. The tick marks below count residues.     The real Kemp eliminase manifest

    repository facts, CC-BY docs

    The shape of one of the six shipped manifests, with the fields this page explains. The real file carries a few more, among them the input motif structure and how many designs to make. motif_atoms names the atoms of each motif residue that the restraint actually holds: for the glutamate, the two carboxylate oxygens and the two carbons behind them, which fixes the direction the group points as well as its place. Everything after design configures the later stages: how many sequences to write, how many seeds to fold, which evaluation suite to run.

    Two choices the language encodes

    repository facts and our own explanation

    Indexed against unindexed. The string above is indexed: the motif groups sit at the sequence positions it gives, with this much chain between them. Its bar sets one thing loose, which of the two groups comes first, so the resolved line above changes from run to run. That is ordering, not freedom of position. Unindexed conditioning is a different setting: the motif residues move to their own field, unindexed_motif_residues, motif_str keeps a single designable run and the ligand chain, and the model decides during generation where the groups land. In theory that freedom should find better placements. The repository reports the opposite in practice: indexed conditioning gives slightly better results, which is why the main Kemp manifest is the indexed one and the unindexed form ships beside it as a variant.

    Full against partial motifs. A full motif states every catalytic group and the molecule they act on. A partial motif states fewer of them, or holds only some atoms of each residue, and leaves the rest to the model. Holding less gives the generator more room to find a shape that folds, at the cost of saying less about the chemistry. The motif_atoms field is where that trade is made concrete: hold a whole side chain and its direction is fixed; hold one atom and the group can swing.

    Why the lengths are sampled. A designed run written as a range is drawn once per design, so a campaign of many designs covers many layouts from one manifest. The chain planner in design.js tries repeatedly until the total lands inside the requested seq_length, and tells you when the range cannot be reached at all. That is a plain sampler, not a model: no learning is involved in deciding how long a loop should be.

    Total length is the sum over segments. Held segments contribute a fixed count; designed runs contribute a draw from their range.

    04

    The lab: grow a protein around the motif

    This is our own simulation, and it is not AlphaProtein Novo. There is no learned model anywhere on this page. The scaffolder below runs reverse diffusion with a hand-written denoiser: at every step it pulls the chain toward protein-like geometry, holds the motif in place, keeps the ligand enclosed, and then adds back a little less noise than it did last step. The shapes it makes are not predictions. What it reproduces faithfully is the structure of the problem: hold a motif, build a plausible chain around it, then find out whether the chain would hold the motif on its own.

    05

    Sequence and self-consistency: the honesty check

    A backbone is not a protein. It is a shape, and it only means something if some chain of amino acids would actually fold into it. So the design is written as a sequence, and then the sequence alone is folded back, with every memory of the design thrown away.

    Sequences for this backbone

    heuristic designer, not LigandMPNN
    Run a design in the lab to write sequences for it.
    The two self-consistency checks appear here once a design has been scored.

    Our designer is a heuristic, not a learned model: it scores each position by how buried it is, how close it sits to the ligand, and what the local shape prefers, then samples from that at a temperature. The motif residues keep their own identity, because their identity is the chemistry. The real pipeline uses LigandMPNN here, which sees the ligand as context and writes far better sequences than any heuristic.

    Why fold it back, and why more than once

    method of the paper, our own words

    The trap. A scaffolder that is told to hold a motif will hold the motif. That tells you nothing: the restraint did the work, not the protein. Remove the restraint, fold the sequence on its own, and a design that was only held together by the clamp falls apart. The gap between the two is the self-consistency check, and the real pipeline measures it with AlphaFold 3, by its default tuned for designs that carry a covalent intermediate.

    Why an ensemble. One sequence can fail for its own reasons and one sequence can get lucky. Writing several sequences for the same backbone and scoring their agreement asks about the backbone rather than about one sequence written for it. The paper names that, with mechanism-based metrics, as what made the designs work.

    TM-score, the standard fold-level comparison: near 1 is the same fold, near 0.3 is unrelated. The length term keeps the scale fair between a small protein and a large one.

    06

    The campaign funnel: make many, keep few

    No single design is the output of this method. A campaign is, and a campaign is a funnel: generate a batch, write sequences for each, fold them, then throw away everything that fails a filter. The filters are where mechanism knowledge enters, and getting them right is what the paper reports as the thing that unlocked the rest.

    Run a campaign

    original simulation, our own filters
    Each design costs about a tenth of a second. Twelve of them run in a few seconds, one at a time, so the page keeps answering a press.
    The campaign runner loads with the lab.

    Each stage of the funnel keeps the designs that pass one filter and drops the rest. The shape of the funnel, not its height, is the thing to watch: a filter that keeps almost everything is not doing any work, and a filter that keeps almost nothing is either the real constraint or badly set.

    What a filter is for

    method of the paper, our own words

    A filter is cheap and the laboratory is not. Everything upstream exists to put a small number of designs in front of an experiment with a decent chance of working, so the only useful filter is one that correlates with what happens in a tube. That is a harder requirement than it sounds: a metric can be perfectly well defined, easy to compute and completely uninformative about catalysis.

    The paper's stated enabler was finding metrics of the right kind. Two things mattered: reading the metric off a structure predicted for the designed sequence rather than off the design itself, and deriving the metric from the reaction mechanism, so it asks whether the chemistry can happen rather than whether the picture looks like a protein.

    Our funnel runs our own filters on our own designs, so its proportions mean nothing about AlphaProtein Novo. What it does show is the mechanic: the cost of one design, the cost of the whole campaign, and how fast a stack of filters cuts a batch down.

    Designs left after the first k filters, if each passes a share p of what reaches it. Stacked filters multiply, which is why a campaign starts large.

    07

    The real pipeline, and what this page leaves out

    Everything in this section is documented in the Apache-2.0 repository. Sections 04 to 06 are our own simulation of the same idea, and the differences are large. Here they are, side by side.

    Four stages, one command each

    repository facts, CC-BY docs

      One run of the pipeline produces a directory per stage, so a design can be traced from the backbone that was generated to the metrics that judged it. The resequencing stage is optional. The folding stage is run once per reaction state and per seed, which is why a campaign with seven states costs several times what a campaign with two costs.

      Diffusion, and starting from a parent

      repository facts, CC-BY docs

      The generator's default schedule runs steps from noise to structure. Partial diffusion starts somewhere in the middle instead: take a design that already works, add noise back to the level of step k, and run the reverse from there. How far back you go sets how much of the parent survives.

      The TM-scores in the table are the real pipeline's, as its README reports them, and they are approximate. They are not measurements of our simulation. Our scaffolder reproduces the direction only: fewer steps back keeps the parent, more steps back frees the design.

      What is licensed, and what is not here

      repository facts, CC-BY docs

      The split matters for a page like this one. The code and its documentation can be read, described and built on. The weights are a separate download under their own terms and are not in the repository; anything the model produced carries its own terms too. The preprint is All rights reserved, so this page states its claims in its own words and links to it rather than quoting it.

      The metrics the repository's README names

      names from the repository, CC-BY docs

      The last column says whether this page computes something of the same shape. A yes means our lab measures the same kind of thing with its own code on its own designs: it does not mean the numbers are comparable. The confidence metrics have no counterpart here at all, because they come out of AlphaFold 3 and there is no learned model on this page to be confident about anything.

      What the lab simplifies

      plainly

      No learned model. The real generator is a diffusion model trained on protein structures. Ours is a hand-written relaxation: springs, repulsion, a compaction term and a secondary-structure preference, with noise on a schedule. It has the same loop shape as reverse diffusion and none of the knowledge.

      No full backbone and no side chains. A design here is one point per residue for the backbone and one for the side-chain direction, plus a fixed ligand. The real model generates every atom, which is what makes a hydrogen bond a hydrogen bond rather than a distance between two dots.

      No AlphaFold 3. The scoring check lets go of the catalytic residues and relaxes the design: a pocket that really cradles the motif hardly moves, and one that only looked right because the residues were nailed down springs apart. That is the lab's own stand-in, not the pipeline's test. We also ran the real test, folding the sequence again from noise, and it carries no signal here: the score sits near 0.20 whatever sequence goes in, because AlphaFold 3 learned how a sequence folds and our denoiser learned nothing. The lab reports that number and marks it as uninformative.

      No chemistry. There is no energy function, no solvent, no electrostatics and no reaction. The transition state is a rigid body with the right shape and nothing happens to it.

      One ligand, one chain, no states. The real campaigns fold several snapshots of the reaction and compare them. Ours has one state: the ligand sits in the pocket and stays there.

      Numbers are ours. Every number the lab shows was computed by the lab, from a design the lab made. None of them is from the paper, and none of them should be read as a claim about AlphaProtein Novo.

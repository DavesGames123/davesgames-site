A million-solar-mass object, found only by its gravity

Light that left a radio galaxy about 10 billion years ago bends around a closer galaxy and smears into a thin arc. One short stretch of that arc is dented. Nothing there shines, but the dent says that about a million Suns of mass sit in the foreground galaxy, within 80 parsecs of one point. That is a hundred times lighter than anything found this way before.

The lens: JVAS B1938+666

An elliptical galaxy at z = 0.881 sits almost exactly in front of a radio galaxy at z = 2.059. Its gravity maps each point of the background sky to one, two or four points on our sky.

The lens equation

paper eqs. 6 and 7

A ray seen at θ left the source plane at β. The deflection α comes from the projected mass, in units of the critical density.

For zl = 0.881, zs = 2.059 and Planck 2015. This page recomputes it as 1.501 × 1011; 1 mas is 7.96 pc at the lens.

The smooth galaxy: an elliptical power law with slope γ (2 is isothermal), plus external shear. The paper also adds m = 3, 4 multipoles.

The lensing scale of a million Suns at this distance is smaller than the 5 mas beam. Section 2 shows why it is still found.

A gap in the arc

Near a critical curve the lens magnifies tangentially by a huge factor. A pull of a fraction of a milli-arcsecond then moves the arc by much more, and a thin arc shows the dent.

The smooth model is the lens without V, but it may absorb any part of V's pull that is uniform or changes linearly across the window: a source shift plus a local change of convergence and shear. What is left is V's own signature at the crossing of the arc and the critical curve. Turn off the beam to see the raw dent. The noise is 1/350 of the arc's peak, as in the VLBI map (34 μJy per beam against about 12 mJy per beam).

Gravitational imaging: a map of missing mass

Gravitational imaging does not assume a shape or a place for a perturber. It adds a free correction δψ to the lensing potential on a grid of pixels, and lets the data say where mass is missing.

Each run adds a curve: the mass of κGI inside radius r of its peak. Grey: the true PJ profile of V. In the paper, six runs (three penalties, two grids) gave 0.83–1.8 × 106 M☉ at 80 pc, so the result was then checked by a parametric fit. Turn off "V in the data" to see what noise alone makes.

How the solver works

paper eqs. 13–18, simplified

Linearise the image in the potential correction. The paper solves for the source S and δψ together; this toy keeps S fixed at the truth.

One Newton step, solved by conjugate gradients, four steps per run. A is blur(−∇S·∇), R penalises κ, its gradient or its curvature.

The correction to the convergence. The paper writes −½∇²ψ in its sign convention. Features above 3σGI count as real.

The extra deflection. A Gaussian prior on δψ makes noise look like a smooth random field, which is why the paper uses a 3σ threshold.

05

Weighing it: why 80 parsecs

A truncated isothermal (pseudo-Jaffe) profile has two numbers, total mass and truncation radius. The data cannot separate them, but they pin the mass inside one radius very well.

Cylindrical mass M(<r) of a PJ profile

With m80 held, every (mtot, rt) pair passes through the same point at 80 pc. Grey curves span rt = 149 ± 18 pc. The paper found 80 pc to be the radius where the enclosed mass and rt are uncorrelated, so it quotes m80 = (1.13 ± 0.04) × 106 M☉. Release the hold to change mtot on its own.

PJ_tidal fixes rt to the tidal radius. For V that gives 53 pc, too compact: the evidence drops by 16. V may sit farther from the galaxy centre along the line of sight than its projected distance.

06

What is it?

The lens tells us mass, size and position. It does not say whether the mass is dark.

How many such clumps should there be?

paper eqs. 19 and 20
WDM particle

Warm dark matter erases small haloes below a half-mode mass Mhm. The expected number μ of subhaloes of 106–107 M☉ in the sensitive area of 1.06 × 10−2 arcsec2 sets P(n ≥ 1) = 1 − e−μ. The paper reports 0.65 for cold dark matter, 0.36 for 9.1 keV and 0.14 for 4.6 keV. This page scales from the cold-dark-matter value with the half-mode mass of Schneider et al. (2012) and gets 0.38 and 0.16: close, not identical. One detection does not separate the models; its density profile might.

Candidates

companion paper, as reported
  • Dark-matter subhaloIts presence is in no strong tension with the expected numbers in cold or warm dark matter. But the best NFW fit is much more concentrated than simulated cold-dark-matter subhaloes, as for A and for the 109 M☉ object in SDSS J0946+1006.
  • Intermediate-mass black holeIts profile is disfavoured at high significance.
  • Globular clusterDisfavoured at high significance.
  • Ultracompact dwarf galaxyA relatively extended one is not ruled out. Deep optical or infrared images could find its light, but the lensed optical arc lies on top of it.

What is real here, and what is a toy

The figures run a small lens model in your browser. They show the mechanisms of the paper, not its data.

Toy. The lens and source parameters, made to look like Fig. 1 (the paper does not list them). Residuals are made in the image plane; the paper fits the 1.7 GHz visibilities directly with PRONTO and uses nested sampling for the evidence. The smooth model may only absorb a linear part of V's pull. The GI solver keeps the source fixed at the truth. So the toy's residuals are larger than the paper's (tens of σ here, more than 5σ there) and its detection floor is too low.

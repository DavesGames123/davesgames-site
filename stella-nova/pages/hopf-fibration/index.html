About

The 3-sphere S³ is the set of unit vectors in ℂ², a sphere in four dimensions. The Hopf map p sends each of its points to a point of the ordinary sphere S². The points that go to one point of S² make a great circle, the fibre over that point. Each point of S³ is on exactly one fibre, so S³ is a union of disjoint circles, one for each point of S². Near any fibre it looks like a product of a disc and a circle. As a whole it is not S² × S¹.

Stereographic projection keeps circles as circles, so each fibre shows as a round circle in the view. The fibre through the projection point goes through infinity and shows as a straight line. Any two fibres are linked once, as two links of a chain. The fibres over one circle of latitude fill a torus, and each of them is a Villarceau circle of that torus. The latitudes give a family of nested tori that fills space. The equator gives the Clifford torus |z₀| = |z₁| = 1/√2.

The 4D rotation turns S³ before the projection. Along multiplies by eia: each fibre turns on itself and the picture keeps its shape, only the stripes move. Fibre to fibre multiplies by a unit quaternion on the right: it sends every fibre onto another fibre, and the base sphere turns with it (the rings on the small sphere). The circles move through the projection point and come back from infinity on the other side. One plane and Two planes are general rotations of ℝ⁴: the circles stay circles and stay linked, but they are no longer fibres of this map.

The fibres over a closed curve on S² make a torus in S³, a Hopf torus. Ulrich Pinkall showed in 1985 that each Hopf torus is flat, and that its area is π times the length of the curve (with this map onto the unit sphere). A latitude gives a round torus, a wavy curve a torus with bulges. The fibres over the vertices of a regular solid are a set of great circles in which every pair links once.

Change the weights and the circle action becomes t ↦ (eiptz₀, eiqtz₁). For coprime p, q it is a Seifert fibration of S³. Each orbit off the two core circles lies on a torus |z₀| = constant and is a (p, q) torus knot; (2, 3) gives trefoils. Two such orbits link pq times. The core circles z₀ = 0 and z₁ = 0 are shorter orbits, the exceptional fibres.

Heinz Hopf described the map in 1931, in Über die Abbildungen der dreidimensionalen Sphäre auf die Kugelfläche (Mathematische Annalen 104). He proved that it cannot be deformed to a constant map. In modern terms it has Hopf invariant 1 and generates π₃(S²) ≅ ℤ.

In physics: the pure states of a qubit are unit vectors (z₀, z₁) in ℂ², and a global phase eit changes no measurement. So the states that are physically the same are a fibre, and the Hopf map sends a state to its point on the Bloch sphere (up to a reflection of one axis, by convention). The same circle bundle carries the field of a Dirac magnetic monopole of the lowest charge. With S³ as the unit quaternions, which cover the rotations of 3D space twice, the fibre over a direction is the set of rotations that take one fixed axis to that direction.

The tubes are drawn on the GPU: a vertex shader puts each vertex on its fibre, rotates it in four dimensions and projects it. The 3D view uses three.js r160 (MIT licence). The maths and the code of this page are original.

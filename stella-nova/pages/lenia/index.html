About

Lenia is a cellular automaton with no grid of on and off cells. Each cell holds a number from 0 to 1, time moves in small steps, and each cell looks at a smooth ring of neighbours out to radius R. Bert Chan made it in 2015–2018 as a continuous form of Conway's Game of Life.

At each step, the kernel K takes a weighted mean of the ring around each cell. That mean is the potential U. The growth function G then turns it into a change: a cell grows when U is near m, within about s, and decays otherwise. The world adds G / T to every cell and clips the result to [0, 1].

From that one rule come hundreds of stable, moving patterns. Chan named and sorted them like a biologist: Orbium glides, Gyrorbium turns, Scutium and Pterifera fly in lines, and some Geminium divide in two. A small change to m or s can kill a creature, make it explode, or turn it into a new one.

Try the growth view: amber is where the world grows, blue is where it decays. A moving creature grows at its front and decays at its back.

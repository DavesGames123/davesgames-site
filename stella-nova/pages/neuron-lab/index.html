How it works

A neuron as a cable. NEURON cuts every branch into short compartments. Each compartment has a membrane (a capacitor and ion channels) and passes current to its neighbours through the axial resistance of the cytoplasm. In the limit this is the cable equation:

Hodgkin and Huxley. The channels in hh.mod give the ion current from three gates. m opens sodium fast, h closes it slowly, n opens potassium:

The Hines method. Each step is implicit (backward Euler, Δt = 0.025 ms). For a tree, the matrix has one entry per compartment and one pair per branch link, so one sweep from the leaves to the soma and one back solve it in O(n):

What to try. Put a clamp on the soma and a probe far out on a dendrite: the spike runs back into the tree and gets smaller (back-propagation with attenuation). Drop the dendrite channels to 0 and it only spreads passively. Put a synapse far out: its bump is small and slow by the time it reaches the soma. Warm the cell and watch the spike get narrow.

This page and NEURON. This is our own small engine in JavaScript after NEURON's method: the same rate equations as hh.mod, NEURON's dt, temperature and v_init defaults, the d_lambda rule for the number of segments, IClamp, Exp2Syn, NetStim and NetCon with delay and weight. The cells are procedural shapes, not reconstructions. For research, use NEURON itself.

How it works

Cells. Each cell is a reduced model: a Hodgkin-Huxley soma (the rates of NEURON's hh.mod) and a passive dendrite. Each cell has one ExpSyn for excitation and one for inhibition; every incoming NetCon adds its weight to that synapse's conductance:

NetCons. When the soma voltage crosses 10 mV from below, each NetCon from that cell queues an event. It arrives after a synaptic delay plus the axon length over the conduction speed. On screen, each streak is one event in flight:

Gamma by PING. Pyramidal cells (E) excite fast interneurons (I). The interneurons fire together and inhibit the pyramids, which stay quiet until the GABA conductance decays. Then they fire again. The period is about the two delays plus a few GABA time constants, so the network sings at gamma frequency (30 to 80 Hz).

The other two. On the ring, each pyramidal cell excites the cells just ahead of it, so a kick runs round as a wave. In the balanced net, strong excitation and stronger inhibition cancel on average, and the cells fire at random times.

This page and NEURON. NEURON builds networks the same way: cells, point process synapses and NetCon objects with threshold, delay and weight, and a queue of events. This page is our own engine in JavaScript after that model, on the CPU. For research, use NEURON (with ParallelContext for big networks).

OUTBREAK — data sources, licences and credits
=============================================

This page is a toy metapopulation model of natural diseases and
public-health policy. It is not a forecast. Every disease value and
every policy effect is approximate and illustrative, picked from the
ranges in the studies below. Package P checked each citation (authors,
year, journal, volume, pages) against Crossref and Europe PMC records on
2026-10-08. The "Fact-check" section at the end lists the notes that the
cited text does not support.

Population nodes
----------------
Natural Earth (https://www.naturalearthdata.com), public domain.
  - data/nodes.json: ne_50m_populated_places_simple (city names,
    positions, pop_max) and ne_50m_admin_0_countries (POP_EST,
    REGION_WB, INCOME_GRP), from
    https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector (geojson/), written
    by tools/build-nodes.py. Each country population is split over its
    largest cities, so a node is a catchment, not the city alone.
    Left out: countries under 150,000 people, and N. Cyprus and
    S. Sudan (no city matched the country code in the 1:50m places).

Disease presets (diseases.js)
-----------------------------
Influenza
  - Biggerstaff M, Cauchemez S, Reed C, Gambhir M, Finelli L 2014.
    Estimates of the reproduction number for seasonal, pandemic, and
    zoonotic influenza: a systematic review of the literature. BMC
    Infect Dis 14:480. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2334-14-480
    Supports: seasonal R0 1.3 (median 1.28, IQR 1.19-1.37); 1918
    median R 1.80 (IQR 1.47-2.27).
  - Carrat F, Vergu E, Ferguson NM, et al. 2008. Time lines of infection
    and disease in human influenza: a review of volunteer challenge
    studies. Am J Epidemiol 167(7):775-785.
    https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwm375
    Supports: latent 2 d, infectious 3 d (shedding about 4-5 days).
  - Mills CE, Robins JM, Lipsitch M 2004. Transmissibility of 1918
    pandemic influenza. Nature 432:904-906.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03063
    Supports: 1918 R0 2.0 (median R less than 3 in 45 US cities;
    median R0 less than 4 after prior immunity).
  - Taubenberger JK, Morens DM 2006. 1918 influenza: the mother of all
    pandemics. Emerg Infect Dis 12(1):15-22.
    https://doi.org/10.3201/eid1201.050979
    Supports: 1918 fatality 0.02 (case fatality above 2.5 %).
Measles
  - Guerra FM, Bolotin S, Lim G, et al. 2017. The basic reproduction
    number (R0) of measles: a systematic review. Lancet Infect Dis
    17(12):e420-e428. https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(17)30307-9
    Supports: R0 15 (the often quoted 12-18).
  - World Health Organization. Measles fact sheet.
    https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/measles
    Supports: latent and infectious periods, vaccine efficacy 0.97.
COVID-19
  - Liu Y, Gayle AA, Wilder-Smith A, Rocklov J 2020. The reproductive
    number of COVID-19 is higher compared to SARS coronavirus. J Travel
    Med 27(2):taaa021. https://doi.org/10.1093/jtm/taaa021
    Supports: R0 2.8 (median 2.79).
  - Brazeau NF, Verity R, Jenks S, et al. 2020. Report 34: COVID-19
    infection fatality ratio estimates from seroprevalence. Imperial
    College London. https://doi.org/10.25561/83545
    Supports: IFR 0.006 (population IFR about 0.2 % to above 1 %).
  - Liu Y, Rocklov J 2021. The reproductive number of the Delta variant
    of SARS-CoV-2 is far higher compared to the ancestral SARS-CoV-2
    virus. J Travel Med 28(7):taab124.
    https://doi.org/10.1093/jtm/taab124
    Supports: variant R0 6 (mean 5.08, range 3.2-8).
  - Lauer SA, Grantz KH, Bi Q, et al. 2020. The incubation period of
    coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) from publicly reported
    confirmed cases: estimation and application. Ann Intern Med
    172(9):577-582. https://doi.org/10.7326/M20-0504
    Supports: incubation median 5.1 d, so latent 3 d with
    presymptomatic spread.
SARS
  - Riley S, Fraser C, Donnelly CA, et al. 2003. Transmission dynamics
    of the etiological agent of SARS in Hong Kong: impact of public
    health interventions. Science 300:1961-1966.
    https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1086478
    Supports: R0 2.5 (2.7 at the start, without superspreading events).
  - Lipsitch M, Cohen T, Cooper B, et al. 2003. Transmission dynamics
    and control of severe acute respiratory syndrome. Science
    300:1966-1970. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1086616
    Supports: R0 about 3 without control.
  - World Health Organization 2003. Summary of probable SARS cases with
    onset of illness from 1 November 2002 to 31 July 2003.
    https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/summary-of-probable-sars-cases-with-onset-of-illness-from-1-november-2002-to-31-july-2003
    Supports: fatality 0.10 (774 deaths in 8096 probable cases, 9.6 %).
Ebola virus disease
  - WHO Ebola Response Team 2014. Ebola virus disease in West Africa:
    the first 9 months of the epidemic and forward projections. N Engl
    J Med 371(16):1481-1495. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1411100
    Supports: R0 1.8 (1.71-2.02 by country); incubation 11.4 d; case
    fatality 70.8 % with known outcome.
  - Uyeki TM, Mehta AK, Davey RT, et al. 2016. Clinical management of
    Ebola virus disease in the United States and Europe. N Engl J Med
    374(7):636-646. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1504874
    Supports: lower fatality with intensive care (18.5 %).
Cholera
  - Mukandavire Z, Liao S, Wang J, et al. 2011. Estimating the

    Zimbabwe. PNAS 108(21):8767-8772.
    https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1019712108

  - Codeco CT 2001. Endemic and epidemic dynamics of cholera: the role

Dengue
  - Favier C, Degallier N, Rosa-Freitas MG, et al. 2006. Early

Plague
  - Dean KR, Krauer F, Walloe L, et al. 2018. Human ectoparasites and the

Fact-check (package P, 2026-10-08)
----------------------------------
Every preset value is inside its stated range, and the ranges agree with
the sources above, except for the items below. Package P does not own
diseases.js or render/*.js, so the owners must change the notes.
  1. diseases.js SOURCES[9] (Riley 2003): the note says "case fatality
     in Hong Kong about 13 %". The Riley paper does not give this value.
     It is from Donnelly CA, Ghani AC, Leung GM, et al. 2003. Lancet
     361:1761-1766, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(03)13410-1
     (13.2 % under 60 years, 43.3 % at 60 or more). The title also
     leaves out ": impact of public health interventions".
  2. diseases.js SOURCES[21] (Lauer 2020): the note says "infectiousness
     starts about 2 days before symptoms". Lauer gives only the
     incubation period. The presymptomatic part is from He X, Lau EHY,
     Wu P, et al. 2020. Nat Med 26:672-675,
     https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-020-0869-5 (infectiousness peaks on
     or before symptom onset; 44 % of transmission before symptoms).
  3. plague R0 1.8, range [1.3, 3]: neither cited source (Dean 2018,
     WHO fact sheet) gives an R0. The nearest primary value is Gani R,
     Leach S 2004. Emerg Infect Dis 10(4):608-614,
     https://doi.org/10.3201/eid1004.030509 (pneumonic plague R0 1.3,
     latent 4.3 d, infectious 2.5 d). Keep 1.8 as illustrative for the
     ectoparasite route, but cite Gani and Leach for the lower bound.
  4. diseases.js SOURCES[2] (Mills 2004): the note says "median about
     2-3 (range 2-4)". The abstract says the median R is less than 3,
     and the median R0 is less than 4 after prior immunity.
  5. diseases.js SOURCES[15] (Favier 2006): the abstract gives no R0
     values. The note "mostly about 2-6" is not verified from the
     abstract.
  6. render/style-night.js, style-marble.js, style-flat.js SOURCES: the
     city lights are "Black Marble 2012" in the notes, but
     ancient-earth/CREDITS.txt says that lights-2k.jpg is Black Marble
     2016. Change "2012" to "2016".
  7. Ebola preset: vaccine exists false, lagDays 365. This is correct for
     2014. rVSV-ZEBOV (Ervebo) has had a licence since 2019, so the
     preset shows the 2014 epidemic. Not an error.

About

This is a toy model of how a natural disease spreads between cities, not a forecast. Each of 395 cities and its catchment is one well-mixed population with the compartments susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered, dead and vaccinated (SEIR, with waning immunity for some diseases). Nearby cities share infection over land; flights move infected travellers between hubs. The flights come from a gravity model of city size and distance, scaled to about 12 million passengers a day, not from real schedules.

The first cases and the first infected flights are random events, so two runs with different seeds differ. Large epidemics are smooth. Vector-borne diseases (dengue, malaria, plague) depend on latitude and season, and cholera on the water reservoir. Some diseases are more often fatal where health care is weaker; the model scales their fatality by the income group of the country.

Every disease value is approximate and illustrative, picked from the ranges in published studies. The policies change the transmission rate, the flights and the land coupling by factors from the literature on non-pharmaceutical interventions. Real outbreaks depend on much that a model of this size leaves out: age, households, testing, behaviour, and treatment.

Cities: Natural Earth populated places (public domain). Coastlines and land: Natural Earth 1:50m (public domain). Earth images: NASA Blue Marble and Black Marble (public domain). Disease and policy values: the studies listed in diseases.js and policies.js, and in CREDITS.txt.

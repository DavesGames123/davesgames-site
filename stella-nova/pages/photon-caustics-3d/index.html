How it works

Each wave crest is a weak lens. A grid of photons covers the water. Each photon refracts by Snell's law at the surface and goes to the floor. A patch of water that maps to a smaller patch of floor makes that floor brighter by the ratio of the two areas. Where crests focus at the depth d, the patches shrink to lines: the bright net on the floor.

The water surface is a height field. Each step, the speed v of each cell moves toward the mean of its four neighbours, so ripples spread out and reflect at the walls. The coupling K = 2s² sets the ripple speed: it is proportional to the wave speed s. The caustics come from the height field of each frame, so they stay correct at any speed.

How it works

Light leaves the source as photons. Each photon goes straight until it meets a surface. A mirror reflects it. At glass or water the Fresnel equations give the chance R that it reflects; else it refracts by Snell's law, with an index n that changes with the wavelength λ.

The light image adds the path of every photon, so a pixel is as bright as the number of photons that cross it. Where the rays of a family come near each other the image is bright. The edge where they touch and fold back is the caustic. On it the Jacobian J of the ray map is zero, and the irradiance E ∝ 1/|J| goes to a peak.

In the pool each wave crest is a weak lens. Where a crest focuses at the depth d, the rays crowd into a bright line on the floor. See the 3D pool for the net of these lines in two dimensions of floor.

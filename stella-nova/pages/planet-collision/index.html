Settling the bodies

This page simulates tens of thousands of particles on the GPU. It needs WebGPU: Chrome or Edge 113+, Safari 26+, or Firefox 141+ on Windows.

What you see

Two bodies made of equal-mass particles: an iron core and a rock mantle (and an ice shell for icy bodies), with their real masses. Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) moves them under their own gravity and pressure. Pressure comes from the Tillotson equation of state of iron, granite, basalt and ice.

The surface is drawn from the particles. The surface map moves with the rock it started on. Hot matter glows with the colour of a black body at its temperature. Vapour is the part of the gas phase that Tillotson marks as expanded and hot. Free fragments are drawn as rocks with motion streaks.

What is simplified

  • 8,000 to 60,000 particles. Research codes use 100,000 to 100 million.
  • Tillotson is a simple equation of state. The model Earth is 6 % smaller than the real one.
  • Temperature is an estimate from the heat added, with a fixed heat capacity. The bodies start cold, so all the heat is impact heat. SPH over-heats the first contact surface.
  • No strength, no atmosphere, no oceans, no cooling. The atmospheres and the impact flash are drawn, not simulated.
  • A debris particle is drawn as 1 to 3 rocks. Only the particle is simulated.
  • Gravity is softened over half a particle spacing. One time step for all particles: time slows at the impact because the shock needs short steps.

Aim a collision

The angle is measured from head-on (0). The speed is in units of the mutual escape speed, the speed two bodies gain when they fall together from far away. The dashed arc is the path of the two centres as points; the real bodies touch first.

What you see

Two bodies made of equal-mass particles: an iron core and a rock mantle (and an ice shell for icy bodies), with their real masses. Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) moves them under their own gravity and pressure. Pressure comes from the Tillotson equation of state of iron, granite, basalt and ice.

The surface is drawn from the particles: each particle adds a smooth density kernel to a volume of cells round the bodies, and the surface is where that density reaches a set level. So the bodies, the neck between them and the splash are one liquid surface. The surface map moves with the rock it started on. Hot matter glows with the colour of a black body at its temperature. The skin of the melt cools and forms a dark crust with glowing cracks in the hours after the impact: that cooling is drawn, not simulated. Vapour is the part of the gas phase that Tillotson marks as expanded and hot. Free debris is drawn as a spray of small molten drops with motion streaks.

The numbers

Every few seconds the page reads all particles back. The largest body is the bound mass that stays on it or falls back. The disk is bound mass on orbits that miss the surface (Moon-forming material, in Moon masses). Escaping mass is not bound to the largest body, including a body that runs on after a hit-and-run. Peak temperature is the 99th percentile of the particles, not the single hottest one.

What is simplified

  • 12,288 particles on a phone or tablet, 32,768 on a computer. Research codes use 100,000 to 100 million.
  • Tillotson is a simple equation of state. The model Earth is 6 % smaller than the real one.
  • Temperature is an estimate from the heat added, with a fixed heat capacity. The bodies start cold, so all the heat is impact heat. SPH over-heats the first contact surface.
  • No strength, no atmosphere, no oceans, no cooling. The atmospheres and the impact flash are drawn, not simulated. Bodies under about 1000 km (Vesta, Ceres) act as fluids here.
  • A debris particle is drawn as a spray of 4 to 10 small drops. Only the particle is simulated.
  • Gravity is softened over half a particle spacing. One time step for all particles: time slows at the impact because the shock needs short steps.
  • A small impactor gets more particles than its mass share, so its particles are lighter.

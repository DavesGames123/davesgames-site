How it grows

shadow in a voxel bud, size by resource v leafy node shed branch

A plant is made of metamers

A shoot is a chain of metamers: an internode, a leaf, and a bud in the axil of the leaf. The tip of each shoot carries a terminal bud. Each spring some buds break and grow new shoots; the others stay dormant, and die after a few years. The page follows the model of Palubicki, Horel, Longay, Runions, Lane, Měch and Prusinkiewicz, Self-organizing tree models for image synthesis (SIGGRAPH 2009), year by year.

Light from a shadow grid

Space is cut into voxels the size of an internode. Each leafy node casts shadow down into a pyramid: a voxel k layers below gets a·b−k, spread over (2k+1)² voxels. A bud gets the light Q = (C − s + a)/C from the shadow s in its voxel. Deep in the crown Q is near zero. In a grove all plants share one grid, so they shade each other.

The resource: Borchert and Honda

The light flows down from the buds to the base (the basipetal pass). The base gets the resource v = αQ, and v flows back up (the acropetal pass). At each branch point the main axis gets vm = v·λQm/(λQm + (1−λ)Ql) and the laterals share the rest. λ is the apical dominance: a spruce has a high λ and a strong leader, an azalea a low λ and many equal stems. A bud with v ≥ 1 grows a shoot of ⌊v⌋ metamers.

Direction, shedding, girth

Each new metamer turns toward the least shade near it (the optimal direction), up (strongest on the leader), level (spruce laterals) or down (willow and birch twigs hang). A branch that gets too little light per metamer dies and falls in the late winter, so the crown is a shell of leaves round bare limbs. Each year the pipe model sets the girth: rn = Σ rchildn, and a trunk never gets thinner.

The year

The seed germinates in spring: a short stem with cotyledons, then the first true leaves. Each later spring the buds break and the shoots extend one metamer after the other. Leaves unfold, take the autumn colour of their species, detach, tumble down and lie as litter until the next spring. Cherry blossom opens before its leaves and its petals drift down. Snow lies on the ground and on the upper side of the limbs in winter. The trunk swells through the summer.

The meadow round the tree

The ground is a plant community, not a carpet. Grass grows in tussocks and swards (a Thomas cluster process); it greens up in spring, sends up seed heads in June, cures, browns and lodges in autumn and lies flat under snow. Gusts cross the meadow as waves. Under the crown the light is low, so the sun grasses and meadow flowers give way to shade grass, ferns and periwinkle as the crown spreads, and the floor shows bare soil and leaf litter. Flowers come in drifts by species and bloom in their own months: celandine in March, buttercups in May, oxeye daisies in June; then the petals fall. Stones follow a power law of sizes, a few boulders with many cobbles and pebbles, grouped in outcrops, with moss on their shaded side. Fallen leaves drift into the hollows and against the stones and rot over the winter. Seeds fall from the tree; in deep shade most seedlings die. Past the meadow the wood begins, with a shrub edge.

Roots: diffusion-limited aggregation

With roots on, a quarter of the soil is cut away. The roots grow with the engine of the Diffusion-Limited Aggregation page, a port of fogleman/dlaf: particles walk at random in the soil and join the roots where they first touch them. The hooks randomStartingPosition, motionVector and shouldReset start the walkers deep and make them drift to the root crown, so the roots grow down. They grow as the plant does.

Colours

Natural shows the scanned bark and leaves. The heatmaps colour each metamer by age (the growing tips glow), branch order, pipe flow, the resource of this year, light, or height. A gradient change recolours the plant at once.

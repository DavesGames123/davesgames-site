What am I looking at?

A planet pulls the near side of a moon a little harder than the far side. That difference, the tide, tries to stretch the moon, and the moon’s own gravity holds it together.

The tide grows fast as the moon comes closer. Inside a distance called the Roche limit, a moon held together only by its own gravity is pulled apart, and its pieces spread into a ring.

Here thousands of grains, each pulled by the planet and by every other grain, and bumping into each other, show it happen. Nothing is scripted.

Saturn’s rings may be what is left of such a moon. Canup (2010) proposed a Titan-sized moon that spiralled in and lost its icy mantle; the “Chrysalis” idea (Wisdom et al. 2022) has a moon torn apart about 160 million years ago. The moon here is a simple icy rubble pile.

Details for physicists

Tide against the moon’s own gravity

How hard the planet’s tide pulls on the moon’s surface, compared with the moon’s own gravity there. A solid sphere fails at 1; a tidally locked one at 2/3; a fluid moon already at 0.14.

Held together, over time

The share of the moon’s mass still bound by its own gravity. Time runs in orbits of the starting orbit.

Energy books of the simulation

How well the simulation keeps its energy: the error as a share of the moon’s binding energy (lower is better; 0.001 is 0.1%).

Where moons break up

Each dot is one run: how much of the moon is left after 3 orbits, against its distance from the planet. Rough and cemented moons survive closer in than loose ones. Distance in planet radii, scaled to equal densities (formula).

Method

Up to 32k grains in WebGPU compute. Contacts every step (spring-dashpot, a friction spring with a Coulomb cap, rolling resistance, cement); self-gravity by a direct sum every 32 steps with a linear extrapolation between sums; the planet as a point mass with J2; kick-drift-kick steps. The spiral comes from a gentle drag on the orbit (tidal decay, much faster than in nature). Heat colour: the energy per kilogram that each grain’s collisions turn into heat (dashpots and sliding friction), fading with a time constant of 1/20 of an orbit.

The moon is drawn about twice Titan’s radius; the Roche limit does not depend on the moon’s size. tests.mjs checks the GPU forces against a CPU reference and the formulas against published values.

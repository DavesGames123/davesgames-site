    About

    The colours are a live fluid simulation on the sphere. A 2D incompressible solver runs on your GPU: semi-Lagrangian advection along great circles, a direct pressure projection (FFT in longitude, a tridiagonal solve per wavenumber), Coriolis with f = 2Ω sin φ, and a vorticity correction that keeps vortices from smearing. It is nudged toward the NOAA GFS 10 m winds, 6-hourly from two days ago to five days ahead, and each named storm adds a vortex built from its advisory (position, maximum wind, 34 kt radii). The flow between frames is the solver's own.

    This is a picture of the weather, not a forecast. The grid is 0.7° on a computer and 1.4° on a phone, so a storm's eye is wider here than in reality; the storm vortex is enlarged to at least 1.6 grid cells.

    Colour scale: wind speed on the magma map, with the Saffir-Simpson thresholds (1-minute sustained wind) marked on the legend. Vorticity is shown as cyclonic (red, turning with the storm) or anticyclonic (blue) in both hemispheres. Pressure is the GFS sea-level pressure, with the storm cores from the advisories.

    Data: NOAA National Hurricane Center (CurrentStorms, GIS cones and tracks), Joint Typhoon Warning Center (warnings), NOAA GFS via AWS open data, NASA EONET v3, Natural Earth land. All public domain. Colour map: matplotlib magma (CC0). Cones for storms outside the NHC basins are derived here from the NHC 2025 track-error radii; they are not agency products.

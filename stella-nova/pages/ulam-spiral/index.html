Write the integers in a square spiral and mark the primes. They are not random: they line up on diagonals, and each diagonal is a quadratic. Pick a shape, pick what to mark, and zoom from one number to millions.

About

Stanisław Ulam drew the spiral in 1963, bored in a meeting. Each diagonal half-line of the spiral is a quadratic 4m² + bm + c; some quadratics give far more primes than others, and the Bateman–Horn constant C(f) predicts how many.

The page sieves the odd numbers to 10⁸ (or 10⁹) in a worker. Past that it tests each number with Miller–Rabin (deterministic bases), so you can fly over 10¹² and beyond. Every shape maps n to its place and back in closed form, so any region draws at once.

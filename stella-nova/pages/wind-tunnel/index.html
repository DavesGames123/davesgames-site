About

The tunnel solves the flow with the lattice Boltzmann method. Each cell holds the density of particles that move to its neighbours (9 directions in 2D, 19 in 3D). On each step they move, then collide toward an equilibrium. Density and velocity come out as sums of those numbers. The result follows the Navier–Stokes equations at low Mach number.

A real cow at 60 km/h has a Reynolds number of millions. A grid this coarse cannot hold the small eddies of that flow, so an eddy-viscosity model (Smagorinsky) takes their effect. The large wake, the separation and the shedding are the resolved part. "Re (grid)" is the Reynolds number that the grid itself resolves.

The drag and lift come from the momentum that the flow gives to the object on each step. Treat them as trends: a rounder nose, a lower roof or a boat tail lowers the drag here as in a real tunnel, but the numbers are not those of a real car or cow.

In 2D the object is its side (or top) silhouette. Legs and wheels let the flow through in 2D, because in 3D the air goes between them.
